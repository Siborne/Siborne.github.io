Fund Classification 101: The Three Armies, Nine Unit Types Framework

2026-05-10
May 11, 2026 01:57 +0800
1740字

No matter how fancy the name, every fund on the market falls into one of three categories. This article uses the 'Three Armies, Nine Unit Types' metaphor to break down the essence of money market funds, bond funds, and equity funds, helping you build a complete mental model of fund classification.

Core insight: Every fund on the market, no matter how creatively named, fits into exactly three categories. Understanding this classification is the prerequisite for entering the market. Most people lose money not because they picked the wrong fund, but because they bought something they didn’t even understand.

Overview: A Personal Finance Account Is Essentially Three Armies

Army Allocation Role Core Mission
Supply Corps 10% ~ 20% Money Market Funds Ensure liquidity — accessible anytime, almost zero loss
Imperial Guard 50% ~ 70% Bond Funds / Fixed Income+ Park 1–3 year idle cash, tolerate minor drawdowns
Shock Troops 10% ~ 40% Equity / Index Funds Park 3–5+ year idle cash, pursue high returns

Once you understand the division of labor among these three armies, you are already ahead of 80% of retail investors. Let’s break down each army’s unit types one by one.

First Army: Supply Corps (10% ~ 20% Allocation)

Its mission is not to generate returns — it is to secure your logistics. Park 1–3 months of living expenses and emergency funds here. Accessible anytime, virtually no loss.

Unit Type: Money Market Funds

  • Common products: Yu’ebao (Alipay), Lingqiantong (WeChat Pay), money market mutual funds
  • Definition: You lend money to banks and the government for a tiny amount of interest
  • Holdings: Entirely bank deposits and central bank bills — zero stocks, zero bonds
  • Annualized return: 1.2% ~ 1.6%
  • Maximum drawdown: Less than 0.1%, essentially zero

⚠️ Note: Most money market funds have a daily instant redemption cap of CNY 10,000. If you need more than that in cash urgently, do not wait until the day you need it to redeem — otherwise you may only get CNY 10,000 out.

The Core Logic of the Supply Corps

The Supply Corps is not about making money at all. Its value lies in:

  1. Emergency access: If something happens and you need cash, regular fund redemptions take 1–3 days, but the Supply Corps settles instantly
  2. Frontline reinforcement: When the Shock Troops are crashing, the Supply Corps can deploy reinforcements — continuously sending supplies to the front so you can keep buying at lower prices

Second Army: Imperial Guard (50% ~ 70% Allocation)

The bulk of your portfolio goes here. Role: park 1–3 years of idle cash, tolerate minor losses.

The Imperial Guard has two main branches: Pure Bond Funds and Fixed Income+.

2.1 Pure Bond Funds (Pure-Blood Imperial Guard)

How do you tell if a fund is a pure bond fund? Open the fund details page and check the holdings — zero stocks, zero convertible bonds means it is pure bond.

Essence: A pile of IOUs. You lend money to the government, banks, and high-quality corporations. They repay principal with interest at maturity.

Pure bond funds are divided into three categories by bond maturity:

Type Bond Maturity Annualized Return Max Drawdown Who It’s For
Short-Term Bond Fund ⭐ Under 1 year 2.5% ~ 3.5% < 1% Best for beginners
Medium/Long-Term Bond Fund 1–10 years 3% ~ 6% 1% ~ 3% Small allocation
Ultra-Long-Term Bond Fund 10+ years Extreme cases 15%+ Extreme cases -5% ~ -10% ❌ Avoid entirely

Short-Term Bond Funds (Best for Beginners)

  • Almost never lose money, more flexible than bank fixed deposits
  • With a 1-year bank fixed deposit, withdrawing early forfeits all interest. Short-term bond funds offer redeem-anytime variants with competitive returns
  • Beginners building a stable portfolio should start with short-term bond funds

Medium/Long-Term Bond Funds

  • Characteristic: better returns during rate-cut cycles. Central bank rate cuts benefit bonds; rate hikes hurt them
  • Can hold a small allocation, but should not be your core position

Ultra-Long-Term Bond Funds (❌ Stay Away)

  • During a rate-cut cycle, annual returns can surge past 15%. During a rate-hike cycle, annual losses can reach -5% to -10%
  • Volatility rivals equities. This is a battlefield where institutions deploy professional teams to track interest rate movements
  • Retail investors have no information edge — interest rate direction alone is enough to keep you guessing

2.2 Fixed Income+ (Mixed Imperial Guard)

Core logic: the main force defends the castle (bond base), while a small detachment attacks (stocks / convertible bonds) to capture some extra returns.

Type Equity Exposure Annualized Return Max Drawdown Characteristics
Primary Bond Hybrid 0% (convertible bonds only) 3.5% ~ 5.5% 2% ~ 4% The most stable entry point in Fixed Income+
Secondary Bond Hybrid ⭐ ≤ 20% 4% ~ 7% 3% ~ 6% Balanced offense/defense — what most people should focus on
Debt-Biased Hybrid ≤ 40%–50% 5% ~ 8%+ 5% ~ 10%+ Advanced tier — beginners stay away

Primary Bond Hybrid

  • Zero stocks, only bonds and convertible bonds
  • Equivalent to pure-blood Imperial Guard + a handful of convertible bond special forces
  • If you want a bit more return than pure bonds but fear volatility, start here

Secondary Bond Hybrid (⭐ Recommended)

  • 80%+ allocated to bonds, up to 20% to stocks and convertible bonds
  • When markets rally, you capture some equity upside. When markets tank, the 80% bond base keeps you afloat
  • Balanced offense and defense — no need to gamble on market direction, more stable for long-term holding

Debt-Biased Hybrid (⚠️ Beginners Stay Away)

  • Up to 40%–50% allocation to stocks
  • Rallies harder than secondary bond hybrids, crashes harder too
  • No longer really Fixed Income+ — only consider if you can accept greater volatility in pursuit of higher returns

Third Army: Shock Troops (10% ~ 40% Allocation)

⚠️ Two rules before entering:

  1. Use only idle cash you will not touch for 3–5+ years
  2. Be mentally prepared to lose it all without heartbreak — if you would feel pain, you need to reduce your position

3.1 Broad-Market Index Funds (⭐ Best for Beginners)

Definition: Track a broad market index. Buy the most representative batch of companies in the market. Earn the market’s overall returns.

  • Common indexes: CSI 300, CSI 500, ChiNext Index, Nasdaq-100, S&P 500
  • Annualized return: 8% ~ 12%
  • Maximum drawdown: 30% ~ 50%

Historical data shows that holding for 3+ years almost never results in a loss.

Why recommend broad-market indexes?

  • Poor companies eventually get eliminated from the index; only the elite remain
  • Buying the index = permanently holding the strongest group of companies in the market, earning from overall economic growth

📖 The Buffett story: In his will, Buffett instructed his wife to put 90% of her inheritance into a low-cost S&P 500 index fund.

Beginners entering the Shock Troops should start with broad-market indexes — do NOT jump into sector funds right away.

3.2 Sector / Thematic Funds (⚠️ High Risk)

Definition: Invest only in stocks of a specific sector or theme. Try to earn from sector cyclical upswings.

  • Common sectors: New energy, semiconductors, healthcare, consumer goods, defense, AI, etc.
  • Volatility: Roughly double that of broad-market indexes
  • Maximum drawdown: During sector downturns, can reach 50% ~ 70%+

Real-world case: New energy funds surged over 100% in 2021, then lost nearly half their value in 2022.

Sector funds are a bet on track logic — bet right and you win big; bet wrong and you lose big.

The threshold question — you must be able to answer:

  • Why is this sector’s cycle trending upward right now?
  • When might it reverse?

If you cannot answer these questions, you should not touch sector funds.

3.3 Equity-Biased Hybrid Funds (Actively Managed Funds)

Definition: 60%–95% allocation to stocks. Fund managers actively pick stocks and adjust the portfolio.

  • Top performers’ annualized return: 15% ~ 20%
  • Maximum drawdown: 40% ~ 60%+

⚠️ The Champion’s Curse: Throughout the history of China’s mutual fund industry, the previous year’s top-performing fund almost always suffers a massive drawdown the following year.

Why does the Champion’s Curse exist?

  1. After winning the crown, massive capital floods in and the fund’s AUM balloons by tens of times → the manager cannot find enough quality targets → excess returns get diluted
  2. The “champion” usually rode a sector tailwind that year — it does not necessarily signal genuine skill

Never buy any fund ranked in the top 10 by 1-year return. The Champion’s Curse has a 100% hit rate.

How to pick instead? Look for fund managers with 3–5+ years of track record who have weathered both bull and bear markets — the kind who consistently beat the index without relying on sector tailwinds.

3.4 QDII Funds (The Overseas Squad)

Definition: Use RMB to buy overseas funds, achieving geographic diversification.

  • Available products: Nasdaq-100, S&P 500, and other index funds
  • Annualized return: US equities 8% ~ 20%, HK equities 5% ~ 20%
  • Maximum drawdown: Varies, 20% ~ 50%+

Core purpose: Geographic diversification. When A-shares fall, you do not lose everything — the two markets do not always move in the same direction.

🚫 Iron rule: Never, ever touch crude oil funds or commodity funds!

Three Ultimate Rules to Avoid Pitfalls

Rule One: Pure Bonds Come in Three Flavors — Short Is Safe, Medium Is Okay, Ultra Is Dangerous

Short-term bond funds are the best entry point for bond investing. Medium/long-term can be a small allocation. Ultra-long-term bonds carry equity-like volatility — retail investors should stay away.

Rule Two: Beginners Should Buy Passive Funds First, Not Active Funds

Passive funds (broad-market indexes) have low fees, do not depend on any individual manager’s skill, and outperform most active funds over the long run. Only consider active funds once you have developed sufficient market judgment.

Rule Three: Funds Recommended by Banks or Apps Are Traps Nine Times Out of Ten

Platforms typically push the funds that have performed best recently — but the best performers are often the next ones to crash. Refer back to Rule Two: the Champion’s Curse spares no one.
